# Early user testing

**What this is:** a small, early usability test of the Merros prototype. It is not a clinical study, it was not powered to show anything statistically, and it used no real patient data.

## Method

* **Participants:** 12 adults.
* **Scenario:** simulated. Each person used the demo couple (Mariam and Khalid, synthetic data) and was asked to read Mariam's result as if it were their own, compare it with a partner using the Blind Couple Match, ask the Genetic Copilot questions, and complete Teach Back.
* **Data:** synthetic results only. No real genetic data, health records or identities were entered.
* **Measures:** whether the person could explain their result correctly in their own words, first without help and then after Teach Back; whether they found the Copilot's answers clear; and whether they would recommend Merros to a friend or relative.

## Results

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| People who tested Merros | 12 |
| Understood their result correctly the first time | 8 of 12 |
| Understood their result correctly after Teach Back | 12 of 12 |
| Found the Copilot's answers clear | 12 of 12 |
| Would recommend Merros to a friend or relative | 12 of 12 |

## What this does and does not show

* Teach Back did its job here: the four people who misunderstood at first got there after the second explanation. This supports keeping Teach Back as a required step.
* Twelve people is a small group, and a simulated scenario is not the same as receiving your own result. Real results carry emotion, family pressure and time that a test cannot reproduce.
* The participants were not selected to represent the UAE population, and the test was not run in Arabic and English in equal numbers.
* These numbers should not be quoted as evidence of clinical effect. The pilot plan (docs/PILOT_PLAN.md) describes the larger evaluation needed for that.
